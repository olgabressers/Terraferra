Positioning Verdict — what changes
  • Answer firstWhy should a designer pay 30% of sales plus $500 a month? In March 2026 Meta launched a free option: creators tag products, keep the full commission the brand sets, and the buyer checks out on the brand's own site — Meta takes nothing (source). Until this page can say what a designer gets for 30% that the free option doesn't, nothing else here matters. Still open — needs a real side-by-side of cost and traffic delivered per platform, which doesn't exist yet.
  • DropStop pitching collections as what makes SMTHG different. Pinterest boards already let people follow, co-curate with others, and comment. ShopMy's Circles already pull several creators' links into one shoppable page. Keep building collections — they still drive traffic — just stop using them as the pitch.
  • Update"Earning continues after the drop" is now a real plan, not a gap. LTK pays creators for 7–30 days after a click, usually 7–14 (source). SMTHG's plan: 45 days, tied to the return window — longer than LTK's. Still open — this is only a plan so far, nothing built or tested yet.
  • RedesignDon't open SMTHG to any creator with no requirements. Flip tried exactly that — no follower minimum, paid per view. It hit 16.5 million users and a $1 billion+ valuation, then shut down in August 2025 because the economics didn't work (source). A gate that lets in the people LTK and ShopMy reject is the right call — removing every gate already failed once.
  • NarrowStop claiming "community" broadly — narrow it to one thing nobody else has: a page each brand owns and posts to. LTK already added public profiles, two-way chat, and content browsing, and now calls itself a social platform with 40 million+ monthly users (source). General "community" is taken. A brand's own page is not.
  • BuildBuild in this order: discount codes, then look feedback, then member matching. First, a discount code per influencer that works on the brand's own store — it survives Safari blocking cookies after 7 days, which most competing tools don't. Second, let members react to a look — no AI needed, works with just five people. Third, match members who saved the same item — start as a manual weekly post, no data system required yet.
  • Build firstBuild AI try-on first — it replaces sending out physical samples. SMTHG never ships samples, so try-on is how an influencer gets to see and post about a product — it stands in for the sample, it's not just a shopper nice-to-have. Still open, and this one matters: FTC rules say you can't describe experience with a product you haven't tried, and a rule in force since October 2024 extends that to fake testimonials — whether an AI-generated try-on photo counts as a "testimonial" has never been tested. Get US legal advice before a drop depends on this.
  • ProveTrack repeat purchases from people who joined the community after a drop. Compare them to buyers who didn't join. That one number proves whether community is worth the cost.
  • ConcedeGive up on beating Pinterest at visual discovery — spend that effort making drop pages readable by AI assistants instead. Pinterest already has 631 million people a month finding products by image; SMTHG's own AI style-matching is still just planned, not built. As more shopping search moves into AI chat, being easy for those AI systems to read is a cheaper fight to win.

02Monetisation Model

SMTHG is the only platform in this set with a single bundled platform commission shown to designers. Every affiliate-model competitor splits the take between a brand-set influencer rate and a separate platform fee; the visual-discovery and wardrobe apps in this set don't monetise commerce directly at all. Two platforms (Wolf & Badger, Farfetch) don't publish a single commission rate — shown as sourced estimate ranges, not fact. Every competitor pays creators in cash on a last-click window of 3 to 30 days; SMTHG's plan adds brand credit or a discount as an alternative reward for members whose audience is too small to earn meaningful cash. The nearest comparable is LTK's Power Gifting, where brands send free product to creators ahead of a partnership (source) — that is product given before the work, not a reward paid for a sale.

04Strategic Conclusion

SMTHG's model combines five elements:
  • Drops sold through influencer promotion — e.g. an Instagram post drives the influencer's audience to buy on smthg.us during the drop window.
  • A dedicated storytelling Space per brand.
  • Member-to-member community discussion.
  • A per-member collection page in the community area, aggregating affiliate links to the regular, ongoing assortment — not to drops, since a drop's links stop converting once the drop ends.
  • A two-step conversion goal: purchase first, optional community membership second.
  • Affiliate-led commerce drives sales the same way it does for LTK and ShopMy — that mechanic stays central to SMTHG's model.
  • Community is the differentiator: brand-owned Spaces and member discussion layered onto that same commerce mechanic — a layer neither LTK nor ShopMy offers.
  • Any community member can build shoppable collections that aggregate affiliate picks from the regular, ongoing assortment — not from drops, since a drop's links stop converting once it ends — the community-native version of LTK/ShopMy's storefront, open to any member rather than a dedicated influencer type, and the piece that completes the community layer.
  • The Zero-to-One launch proves the combination: drops generate attributable sales through influencer-driven traffic, and drop buyers convert into community members.

05Where to Focus

SMTHG differentiates in practice only if community drives repeat purchase — that is the one number the launch needs to prove.
  • Any community member can build collections (Pinterest-style boards) of product picks — no dedicated "Influencer" profile type or space. Affiliates use collections as their traffic tool; everyone else uses them for style curation.
  • Affiliate sales are attributed by link marker: a marker carries from an external collection link to the ecom page, and through a cookie window if the buyer later returns as a community member. No marker on the path means no affiliate credit, regardless of who curated the collection.
  • Brand Spaces need to be genuinely active with each brand's own founder/designer content — story, behind-the-scenes, upcoming drops.
  • A friend referral advances the referrer's membership status.
  • Track whether members who join after a drop come back and buy again — that repeat-purchase number is the actual test of the differentiation claim, since it's what LTK and ShopMy's affiliate-only model does not produce.

06Assortment — Drops + Regular Listings

  • Drops stay the primary mechanic: they establish SMTHG's fashion-specific identity and remain what draws the audience in. The platform cannot rely on drops alone.
  • Designers can also list a regular, always-available assortment on the platform — the same model as a standard multi-brand marketplace (e.g. Farfetch) — running alongside drops.
  • Regular assortment gives influencers ongoing product to build looks from between drops, rather than only when a drop is live.
  • Regular assortment gives SMTHG a second, continuous sales channel beyond the intermittent timing of drop windows.

07What to Watch Out For

The biggest single risk is the take rate, not any one competitor. Meta charges 0% (via Instagram Shop) vs. SMTHG's 30% + $500/mo. Pinterest is the discovery risk; LTK closed the community gap in Jul 2025. The real question: what does a designer get for 30% that a free channel doesn't?
