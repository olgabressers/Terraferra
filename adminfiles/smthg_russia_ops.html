Cross-Border Shipments — MVP
Russia, EU, UK, Turkey, Japan, and the US as brand origins, shipping to the US, EU, and UK. Research current as of August 2026.
De minimis is gone. Section 321 (duty-free, paperwork-free under $800) was suspended for all countries — 29 Aug 2025, codified by CBP 24 Jun 2026, no restoration date. Every US-bound shipment on this page now needs a customs entry with CBP, even at low value. A broker is optional and a bond is not required at typical per-item order values (under $2,500). EU VAT (IOSS) Registration — Steps, Timeline, Cost
Conclusion: not difficult — it is a standardized, outsourced process. The real cost of taking it on is that SMTHG becomes the compliance-holding party for EU sales, which is what disqualifies the EU/UK routes from the zero-SMTHG-paperwork MVP bar above — the process itself is straightforward.
What it isThe EU's simplified VAT scheme for consignments of €150 or less. As the platform collecting payment, SMTHG is the party that registers.
Steps1) Appoint an EU-based IOSS intermediary — a non-EU business cannot register directly. 2) Provide company details (legal name, address, responsible person's ID). 3) The intermediary registers SMTHG in one EU member state (commonly Ireland, Netherlands, or Germany) — one registration covers all 27 EU countries. 4) Configure checkout to charge the correct local EU VAT rate per destination country. 5) File a return through the intermediary every month.
TimelineA few weeks from appointing the intermediary to an active IOSS number.
Setup costRoughly 200 to 500 EUR (approximately $218–545) one time, where charged — document verification, tax authority registration, IOSS number issuance. Some providers fold this into the first month's fee instead of billing it separately.
Monthly costDriven by transaction volume rather than a flat rate. EAS Project (the only provider with fully published pricing): 9.90 EUR/month for up to 10 transactions, 79 EUR/month for up to 100, 329 EUR/month for up to 1,000, 590 EUR/month for up to 5,000 (approximately $11–643/month across that range). At SMTHG's MVP-stage EU order volume, the real cost sits at the low end of that range.
Why it cannot be a one-time paymentA non-EU business is legally barred from self-filing — the intermediary is jointly liable for the VAT and must remain in place for as long as the registration is active. A return is required every month.
Sources: EAS Project — IOSS registration ↗ · GoodVat — IOSS intermediary pricing comparison ↗ · EU VAT One Stop Shop — filing deadlines ↗
Returns — US Customer Pays Freight and Duty
When a US customer returns an item, the customer books and pays return freight, and pays any duty on the returned item. SMTHG and the brand are not the payer on this leg.
Key operational risk: a slow or expensive return path pushes US customers toward a chargeback instead of a return — disputing the charge with their card issuer rather than shipping the item back. Chargebacks are routine in US ecommerce, cost the brand the item and the sale, and carry an additional processing fee.
Idea, post-MVP: a sustainability game for the community. All US returns are consolidated in a single US hub. Once a quarter, the community can buy items from that hub at a discount.

01 · Customs & Duty — by Route
One rule covers every US-bound route on this page, stated once here: Russia→US, EU→US, UK→US, and Japan→US all require a customs entry with CBP on every shipment regardless of value. Individual fashion items are almost always under the $2,500 informal-entry threshold, which does not require a customs bond — exact breakdown in §02. What differs by route is the duty rate and whether a trade agreement exists, shown below.

Jewelry — Brand-Specific Import Requirements
Reference example: Rod Almayate ↗, an Italian-made luxury jewelry brand based in Spain. Exact materials per piece were not confirmed from the brand's site — the requirements below are general, by material category.
Conclusion: jewelry clears US customs under a different HS chapter than apparel, at a lower duty rate, but with two requirements apparel does not carry — a detailed material/value breakdown on the invoice, and a physical country-of-origin mark on the piece itself, in addition to the package.
Duty rateHS Chapter 71 (precious metal and imitation jewelry) — lower than the 12–20% apparel MFN rate used elsewhere on this page. Gold jewelry: 5.5–6.5%. Silver jewelry: 5.0–8.0%. Precious-stone jewelry: 3.0–5.5%. Imitation/costume jewelry: 7.0–11.0%. Duty is assessed on the full article — materials, labor, and gemstones together, rather than on precious-metal content alone.
Invoice detailBeyond the standard commercial invoice, US customs requires material composition and purity/fineness, net weight, and unit value per piece, with cost of materials, labor, and any royalties or licensing fees broken out separately.
Physical markingEach piece must be marked with its country of origin directly on the item, in a conspicuous and legible way, on top of any marking on the package or hangtag. Small pieces where this is impractical need separate approval.
Rough diamondsNot applicable to finished jewelry. The Kimberley Process (Clean Diamond Trade Act) covers only rough, uncut diamonds crossing a border — it does not certify finished pieces, even ones set with diamonds.
Extra scrutinyCBP gives jewelry shipments more attention than apparel for brand/design intellectual property, conflict-mineral sourcing documentation, and classification accuracy — with a 5-year record-keeping requirement from the entry date.

02 · Customs Entry — Who Files It, What It Costs
Conclusion: Under 19 CFR 142.4, a shipment valued at $2,500 or less clears as an informal entry — no customs bond required, and no legal requirement to hire a broker. A shipment over $2,500 is a formal entry and does require a bond. Individual fashion items are almost always under $2,500, so this is the informal case on every route unless an order bundles multiple high-value pieces.

06 · Turkey → EU & US
Conclusion: Same brand, two completely different outcomes depending on destination. Route Turkish brands to the EU; the US route carries 35–37% combined tariffs.
Turkey → EU0% duty — full customs union since 1995. One document (A.TR certificate) instead of an origin cert. Without it: standard MFN 3–20%+.
Turkey → USNo Free Trade Agreement (FTA) with the US. MFN 12–14.5% + 12.5% Section 301 (Turkish apparel specifically) + 10% Section 122 ≈ 35–37% combined. Excluded from the 2026 textile quota relief given to other countries.

Conclusion: Same pattern as Turkey — free into both the EU and UK, standard MFN duty into the US, freight priced via Japan Post.

09 · Order Flow
Worked example using Russia → US — same flow for every origin, only the carrier and payout account change.
#Step
1Customer pays on the platform. Stripe collects payment + US sales tax (Stripe Tax, automatic).
2SMTHG notifies the brand of the order.
3Brand packs the item and books DHL.
4DHL generates the invoice, packing list, and waybill, and files the customs entry (broker included, §02).
5Brand ships direct to the customer. SMTHG never touches the goods.
6Stripe splits the payment — brand's share to their payout account (non-Russian account required for Russia-based brands), SMTHG keeps commission.
