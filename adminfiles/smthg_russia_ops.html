Cross-Border Shipments — MVP
Russia, EU, UK, Turkey, Japan, and the US as brand origins, shipping to the US, EU, and UK. Research current as of August 2026.
De minimis is gone. Section 321 (duty-free, paperwork-free under $800) was suspended for all countries — 29 Aug 2025, codified by CBP 24 Jun 2026, no restoration date. Every US-bound shipment on this page now needs a customs entry with CBP, even at low value. A broker is optional and a bond is not required at typical per-item order values (under $2,500). Detail in §02.

Which Countries Work for MVP — Right Now
MVP requirement: SMTHG holds zero registrations and files zero paperwork. All VAT, customs, and duty obligations sit on the brand. This table is the MVP gate. Full detail for each route is in the sections below.
Broker, with vs. without: under $2,500 per shipment, hiring a customs broker is optional, no law requires it. Without one, the brand files the customs entry itself through CBP's online system (ACE) or at a port of entry — no fee, but it takes brand time on every shipment. With one, the broker handles the filing for $75–150 per shipment. DHL, FedEx, and UPS already include a broker in their price automatically; PostNL, Royal Mail, Russian Post, and Japan Post do not — the brand chooses either path with those carriers. The landed-cost column below assumes the brand self-files (no broker fee); add $75–150 per shipment if it hires one instead.

Why EU/UK destinations fail on paperwork alone: the moment SMTHG collects payment on-platform, EU and UK law make the platform the VAT deemed supplier for that sale — SMTHG becomes the party legally required to register and file, regardless of the 0% customs duty. Keeping these routes at zero SMTHG paperwork requires the brand to run its own checkout, with SMTHG never touching the payment. That is a different model from the current on-platform checkout.

01 · Customs & Duty — by Route
One rule covers every US-bound route on this page, stated once here: Russia→US, EU→US, UK→US, and Japan→US all require a customs entry with CBP on every shipment regardless of value. Individual fashion items are almost always under the $2,500 informal-entry threshold, which does not require a customs bond — exact breakdown in §02. What differs by route is the duty rate and whether a trade agreement exists, shown below.
FromToDuty & trade agreement status
RussiaUS~12–20% MFN — no Free Trade Agreement (FTA — a treaty that would lower the tariff) between Russia and the US, no origin certificate exists
EUUS~12–20% MFN — no Free Trade Agreement (FTA) between the EU and the US
UKUS~12–20% MFN — no Free Trade Agreement (FTA) between the UK and the US
TurkeyEU0% — EU-Turkey customs union (in force since 1995). Requires an A.TR certificate (a status document, distinct from an origin certificate). Excludes agricultural goods.
TurkeyUSMFN 12–14.5% + 12.5% Section 301 (Turkish apparel specifically) + 10% Section 122 ≈ 35–37% combined. No Free Trade Agreement (FTA) with the US. Excluded from the 2026 textile quota relief given to Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia.
JapanEU0% — EU-Japan Economic Partnership Agreement (EPA), in force Feb 2019. Requires origin proof (EUR.1 certificate or invoice declaration).
JapanUK0% — UK-Japan Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA), in force Jan 2021. Requires origin proof (EUR.1 certificate or invoice declaration).

02 · Customs Entry — Who Files It, What It Costs
Conclusion: Under 19 CFR 142.4, a shipment valued at $2,500 or less clears as an informal entry — no customs bond required, and no legal requirement to hire a broker. A shipment over $2,500 is a formal entry and does require a bond. Individual fashion items are almost always under $2,500, so this is the informal case on every route unless an order bundles multiple high-value pieces.

03 · Russia → US
Conclusion: Not in MVP by default — DHL is the only fully-priced, fully reliable door-to-door option and $540–720 freight + ~12–20% duty breaks the economics for any item under roughly $1,000. Forwarder and Boxberry freight ($20–100) is not blocked by broker or bond cost — under $2,500 per shipment, neither is required. The blocker is that neither has been quoted for a real parcel or verified for delivery speed. Exception: a brand qualifies if it holds a non-Russian bank account (required regardless, see Payout row) and can confirm one of these cheaper options for its own shipment.
Outbound carrierDHL Express — $540 (office drop-off) to $720 (pickup), 7 business days, broker included
Freight forwarder / proxy shippingSpecialized forwarding and consolidation warehouses routing out of Russia: approximately 65 to 90 EUR (approximately $70–100) for a 1.5–2 kg parcel to the United States. Falls under the "national post or regional courier" row in §02 — no bond required (order value under $2,500), broker optional.
Cheaper, unverifiedBoxberry from ~$20, 12–25 days. Not yet quoted for a real parcel; falls under the "national post or regional courier" row in §02 — no bond required, broker optional.
Russian Post — customsRate varies by exact weight, ground vs air-routed transit, and tracking add-on. Small Packet/standard parcel up to 2 kg via local post office; EMS Russian Post (expedited, up to 20–30 kg) starts around 20–40 EUR base before international weight scaling. 15–90 days transit, too slow for the MVP window. US mail shipments clear through CBP's postal informal entry process (effective 24 Jul 2026). Filer is the owner/purchaser (self-filing via ACE or at the port of entry) or a licensed broker hired for convenience — no bond required, order value under $2,500.
ReturnsDHL/FedEx/UPS do not accept inbound-to-Russia shipments (suspended since 2022). Use CDEK Global — $27, 4–6 days.
PayoutBrand needs a non-Russian bank account (Georgian, Armenian, or EU) — Russian accounts cannot receive Stripe/Wise payouts.

04 · EU → US
Conclusion: ~12–20% MFN duty applies — no US-EU free trade agreement. DHL, FedEx, and UPS all operate normally on this route with no restrictions.
Duty~12–20% MFN — no US-EU Free Trade Agreement (FTA) exists
CarriersDHL, FedEx, UPS all operate normally — no restrictions on this route, broker included in their price. PostNL is also available but is a national postal service outside this group — no bond required either way (order value under $2,500), broker optional if using PostNL, see §02.

PostNL standard parcel (over letterbox size, up to 2 kg): from 21.00 EUR (from approximately $23). FedEx or UPS economy service via a discount broker (e.g. ParcelParcel): 30.00 to 38.00 EUR (approximately $33–41). FedEx or UPS express priority service (1 to 3 days): 45.00 to 85.00 EUR (approximately $49–93).

06 · Turkey → EU & US
Conclusion: Same brand, two completely different outcomes depending on destination. Route Turkish brands to the EU; the US route carries 35–37% combined tariffs.
Turkey → EU0% duty — full customs union since 1995. One document (A.TR certificate) instead of an origin cert. Without it: standard MFN 3–20%+.
Turkey → USNo Free Trade Agreement (FTA) with the US. MFN 12–14.5% + 12.5% Section 301 (Turkish apparel specifically) + 10% Section 122 ≈ 35–37% combined. Excluded from the 2026 textile quota relief given to other countries.
07 · Japan → EU, UK & US
Conclusion: Same pattern as Turkey — free into both the EU and UK, standard-rate and unpriced into the US. Best-documented alternative origin on this page after Turkey.
Japan → EU0% duty — EU-Japan Economic Partnership Agreement (EPA), in force Feb 2019. Origin proof required (EUR.1 certificate or invoice declaration).
Japan → UK0% duty — UK-Japan Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA), in force Jan 2021. Origin proof required (EUR.1 certificate or invoice declaration).
Japan → USNo Free Trade Agreement (FTA) with the US. ~12–20% MFN. Informal customs entry required on every shipment — no bond, order value under $2,500 (§02). Japan Post EMS: 5,300 JPY (~$33.28) for 1 kg, 7,900 to 10,350 JPY (~$49.61–65.00) for 2 kg, 3–5 days. Japan Post Airmail/International Parcel: 3,850 to 6,000 JPY (~$24.18–37.68), 5–10 days. Japan Post Surface Mail: ~2,980 JPY (~$18.71) for 2 kg, 1–3 months. Japan Post is a national postal service — hiring a broker there is optional, see §02. Private courier (FedEx/DHL, broker included): $45–75 (~6,000–11,000 JPY).

08 · Domestic US Shipping
Conclusion: No customs, no broker, no bond — applies to a US-based brand shipping to a US customer, and to any return/reship leg once a package has already cleared US customs.
Freight costUSPS Ground Advantage: 7.90 to 10.20 USD retail, scaling by weight and zone. USPS Priority Mail (2–3 days): 11.00 to 15.66 USD retail, lower commercial rates via Click-N-Ship. UPS Ground / FedEx Ground: 9.75 to 15.00 USD published list rate for a 2 lb package, depending on distance; online shipping software can discount below this.
09 · Order Flow
Worked example using Russia → US — same flow for every origin, only the carrier and payout account change.
#Step
1Customer pays on the platform. Stripe collects payment + US sales tax (Stripe Tax, automatic).
2SMTHG notifies the brand of the order.
3Brand packs the item and books DHL.
4DHL generates the invoice, packing list, and waybill, and files the customs entry (broker included, §02).
5Brand ships direct to the customer. SMTHG never touches the goods.
6Stripe splits the payment — brand's share to their payout account (non-Russian account required for Russia-based brands), SMTHG keeps commission.
