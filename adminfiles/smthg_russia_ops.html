Cross-Border Shipments — MVP
Russia, EU, UK, Turkey, and Japan → US and EU. Research current as of August 2026.
De minimis is gone. Section 321 (duty-free, paperwork-free under $800) was suspended for all countries — 29 Aug 2025, codified by CBP 24 Jun 2026, no restoration date. Every US-bound shipment on this page now needs a customs entry, a broker, and a bond. Detail in §02.

Why EU/UK destinations fail on paperwork alone: the moment SMTHG collects payment on-platform, EU and UK law make the platform the VAT deemed supplier for that sale — SMTHG becomes the party legally required to register and file, regardless of the 0% customs duty. The only way to keep these routes at zero SMTHG paperwork is for the brand to run its own checkout (SMTHG never touches the payment) — not the current on-platform model.

01 · Customs & Duty — by Route
One rule covers every US-bound route on this page, stated once here: Russia→US, EU→US, UK→US, and Japan→US all require informal customs entry on every shipment regardless of value, plus a broker and a bond — exact cost breakdown in §02. What differs by route is the duty rate and whether a trade agreement exists, shown below.
FromToDuty & trade agreement status
RussiaUS~12–20% MFN — no Free Trade Agreement (FTA — a treaty that would lower the tariff) between Russia and the US, no origin certificate exists
EUUS~12–20% MFN — no Free Trade Agreement (FTA) between the EU and the US
UKUS~12–20% MFN — no Free Trade Agreement (FTA) between the UK and the US
TurkeyEU0% — EU-Turkey customs union (in force since 1995). Requires an A.TR certificate (a status document, not an origin cert). Excludes agricultural goods.
TurkeyUSMFN 12–14.5% + 12.5% Section 301 (Turkish apparel specifically) + 10% Section 122 ≈ 35–37% combined. No Free Trade Agreement (FTA) with the US. Excluded from the 2026 textile quota relief given to Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia.
JapanEU0% — EU-Japan Economic Partnership Agreement (EPA), in force Feb 2019. Requires origin proof (EUR.1 certificate or invoice declaration).
JapanUK0% — UK-Japan Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA), in force Jan 2021. Requires origin proof (EUR.1 certificate or invoice declaration).
JapanUS~12–20% MFN — no Free Trade Agreement (FTA) between Japan and the US

02 · Broker & Bond — Who Arranges It, What It Costs
Conclusion: Ship with DHL, FedEx, or UPS and this problem is eliminated — it is bundled into their price automatically. Any other carrier requires sourcing a broker and a bond separately. Applies to every US-bound route; Turkey→EU does not require this at all.
Carrier usedWho files with CBPBond costBroker feeWho arranges it
DHL, FedEx, or UPS The carrier — all three are licensed US customs brokers $0 — covered by the carrier's own bond Folded into the carrier's clearance charge (confirmed for RU→US DHL: $540–720 total; not confirmed for EU/UK/Turkey) Nobody — automatic
Any other carrier (Boxberry, Russian Post, national post, regional couriers) TF or the brand, as importer of record $50–100 single shipment, or ~$500–700/year continuous $75–150 per shipment if a broker is hired TF or the brand, every shipment
Global-e / similar MoR: not justified yet. Would remove this entirely, but does not support multi-vendor drop-ship or Russia as an origin, and prices on an opaque custom revenue-share. Reconsider at real volume.

03 · Russia → US
Conclusion: DHL is the only fully-priced door-to-door option — $540–720 freight + ~12–20% duty breaks the economics for any item under roughly $1,000. Forwarder and postal routes run far cheaper but are slower and their broker/bond arrangement is unconfirmed.
Outbound carrierDHL Express — $540 (office drop-off) to $720 (pickup), 7 business days, broker included
Freight forwarder / proxy shippingSpecialized forwarding and consolidation warehouses routing out of Russia: approximately 65 to 90 EUR (approximately $70–100) for a 1.5–2 kg parcel to the United States. Broker/bond arrangement unconfirmed — falls under the "any other carrier" row in §02.
Cheaper, unverifiedBoxberry from ~$20, 12–25 days — not yet quoted for a real parcel, broker/bond arrangement unconfirmed (falls under the "any other carrier" row in §02).
Russian Post — customsRate depends on exact weight, ground vs air-routed transit, and tracking add-on — not a single fixed number. Small Packet/standard parcel up to 2 kg via local post office; EMS Russian Post (expedited, up to 20–30 kg) starts around 20–40 EUR base before international weight scaling. 15–90 days transit, too slow for the MVP window. Customs mechanism differs from Boxberry: US mail shipments clear through CBP's postal informal entry process (effective 24 Jul 2026), not general freight entry. Filer must still be the owner/purchaser or a licensed broker, and a customs bond is still required — same underlying obligation as §02, via a different filing channel.
ReturnsDHL/FedEx/UPS do not accept inbound-to-Russia shipments (suspended since 2022). Use CDEK Global — $27, 4–6 days.
PayoutBrand needs a non-Russian bank account (Georgian, Armenian, or EU) — Russian accounts cannot receive Stripe/Wise payouts.
Sources: DHL Express Russia live calculator (express.dhl.ru), 13 Aug 2026 · Forwarder/proxy-shipping and Russian Post/EMS rate ranges from third-party shipping comparison research, 14 Aug 2026 — not independently verified against a named provider's live calculator

06 · Turkey → EU & US
Conclusion: Same brand, two completely different outcomes depending on destination. Route Turkish brands to the EU, not the US.
Turkey → EU0% duty — full customs union since 1995. One document (A.TR certificate) instead of an origin cert. Without it: standard MFN 3–20%+.
Turkey → USNo Free Trade Agreement (FTA) with the US. MFN 12–14.5% + 12.5% Section 301 (Turkish apparel specifically) + 10% Section 122 ≈ 35–37% combined. Excluded from the 2026 textile quota relief given to other countries.
07 · Japan → EU, UK & US
Conclusion: Same pattern as Turkey — free into both the EU and UK, standard-rate and unpriced into the US. Best-documented alternative origin on this page after Turkey.
Japan → EU0% duty — EU-Japan Economic Partnership Agreement (EPA), in force Feb 2019. Origin proof required (EUR.1 certificate or invoice declaration).
Japan → UK0% duty — UK-Japan Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA), in force Jan 2021. Origin proof required (EUR.1 certificate or invoice declaration).
Japan → USNo Free Trade Agreement (FTA) with the US. ~12–20% MFN. Informal customs entry required, broker and bond required — exact cost breakdown in §02. Japan Post EMS: 5,300 JPY (~$33.28) for 1 kg, 7,900 to 10,350 JPY (~$49.61–65.00) for 2 kg, 3–5 days. Japan Post Airmail/International Parcel: 3,850 to 6,000 JPY (~$24.18–37.68), 5–10 days. Japan Post Surface Mail: ~2,980 JPY (~$18.71) for 2 kg, 1–3 months. Private courier (FedEx/DHL, broker included): $45–75 (~6,000–11,000 JPY).

08 · Order Flow
Worked example using Russia → US — same flow for every origin, only the carrier and payout account change.
#Step
1Customer pays on the platform. Stripe collects payment + US sales tax (Stripe Tax, automatic).
2SMTHG notifies the brand of the order.
3Brand packs the item and books DHL.
4DHL generates the invoice, packing list, and waybill, and files the customs entry (broker included, §02).
5Brand ships direct to the customer. SMTHG never touches the goods.
6Stripe splits the payment — brand's share to their payout account (non-Russian account required for Russia-based brands), SMTHG keeps commission.
