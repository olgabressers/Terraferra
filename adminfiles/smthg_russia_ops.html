01Customs & Compliance — by Route
RU→US and EU→US carry the same customs logic: neither has a US free trade agreement, so both fall under standard MFN tariff treatment. Below $800 goods value, both clear automatically with zero formal customs entry.

02Marketplace vs Affiliate — Which Model for This Route
TF can operate as a marketplace (owns the transaction, collects payment, handles tax, pays co-creator revenue shares automatically) or as an affiliate network (links out to the brand's own site, brand handles checkout and compliance, TF earns a tracked commission). Cross-border routes into the EU/UK force the affiliate model because TF becomes the VAT deemed supplier the moment it collects payment. The US route does not have this problem — the $800 de minimis means there's nothing for TF to be deemed supplier of below that value.

04Order Flow
1
Customer pays on the SMTHG platform. Stripe collects payment and US sales tax (Stripe Tax, automatic toggle).
2
SMTHG notifies the Russian brand of the order — standard drop-ship notification, same as any other brand on the platform.
3
Brand packs the item and books DHL themselves via the DHL Express Russia site or office.
4
DHL generates the commercial invoice, packing list, and waybill automatically at booking, and clears US customs itself under the $800 de minimis — no formal entry, no license.
5
Brand ships direct to the US customer. SMTHG never touches the physical goods.
6
Stripe splits the payment — brand's share goes to their non-Russian bank account (Georgian, Armenian, or EU — required since Russian accounts can't receive Stripe/Wise payouts), SMTHG keeps commission.
05Returns (US Consumer → Russia Brand)
