Code of Ethics

smthg.

This Code of Ethics describes how we ask everyone on smthg. to treat each other and do business — Designers, Influencers, and SMTHG itself. We built it because we care about who we work with, not just what they sell, and we expect the same care in return. A breach of this Code is grounds for suspension or termination under the agreement that applies to them.

1. What We Ask of Everyone

We ask everyone on smthg. to be honest about who they are, what they're selling, and how they represent it — and to comply with the law that applies to them. We don't work with counterfeit goods or anyone who infringes another party's intellectual property, and doing so is grounds for terminating a Designer's or Influencer's relationship with SMTHG. We expect respectful conduct from everyone, with no harassment, discrimination, or abusive behavior toward anyone else on smthg. We don't tolerate bribes, kickbacks, or other improper payments used to gain an advantage on the platform, and we ask everyone to protect any non-public information they access through it, including other people's data.

2. What We Ask of Designers

We work with independent designers we're proud to stand behind, and we ask a few things in return.

Fair labor. Everyone in your supply chain must work in a safe environment, be paid at least the applicable minimum wage, and work reasonable hours — and your supply chain must be free of trafficked, bonded, forced, or child labor, at every step. US law bans the import of any good made with forced labor (Tariff Act of 1930, 19 U.S.C. § 1307), and goods linked to the Xinjiang region carry an additional, rebuttable presumption of forced-labor origin under the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA) — you're responsible for making sure nothing you list falls under either ban.

Inclusive brands. We work with brands that welcome and represent people of all backgrounds, and we expect the same commitment to inclusion from every designer we feature.

Animal welfare. We don't accept farmed fur, exotic skins, or feathers. Hides are only acceptable when they're a co-product of the meat industry — never from an animal killed for its skin alone. Any animal-derived material must comply with applicable US wildlife-import law, such as the Lacey Act (16 U.S.C. § 3372), and you must be able to provide documentation for it on request.

Sustainable materials. If you work with endangered or exotic hardwoods, use off-cuts or recycled wood, and never source from illegal logging or deforestation — the same Lacey Act (16 U.S.C. § 3372(f)) that governs animal materials above also bans importing illegally sourced plant and wood products.

Claims you can back up. Before you make a claim like "sustainable," "ethical," "handmade," or "eco-friendly," you need competent and reliable evidence for it in hand — not after the fact. Broad, unqualified claims are exactly what the Federal Trade Commission's (FTC — the US consumer-protection agency) Green Guides (16 CFR Part 260) warn against: say what you actually mean, e.g. "made from 80% recycled cotton" rather than just "sustainable." An unsupported claim is an unfair or deceptive act or practice under Section 5 of the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. § 45). Keep your substantiation on file and provide it to us on request.

Product safety and labeling. Comply with applicable US product-safety law — including the Flammable Fabrics Act and the Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act (CPSIA) — and with textile fiber content, country-of-origin, and care-labeling requirements, as set out further in the Designer Terms & Conditions.

Honest about timing. Because smthg. runs on Drops rather than held inventory, the FTC's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule (16 CFR Part 435) applies to any delay beyond what you disclosed at the time of sale — tell customers a realistic ship date, and tell us and them promptly if a Drop is going to ship later than promised.

US legal compliance. You're responsible for making sure every product you offer through smthg. complies with applicable US federal, state, and local law — covering product safety, labeling, advertising, environmental claims, intellectual property, forced labor, imports, and consumer protection. We may ask for reasonable evidence of compliance, and may suspend or remove a listing where compliance can't be shown.

3. What We Ask of Influencers

We're glad to have you telling our designers' stories, and we ask you to do it honestly.

Disclose paid relationships. Clearly and conspicuously disclose every sponsored or compensated post, every time, as required by the FTC's Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) — the disclosure needs to be easy to notice, not buried behind "more" or a wall of hashtags.

Represent it accurately. Whether you're showing a product via virtual try-on or in person, show it as it actually is, and never post a fabricated or misleading result.

Respectful community conduct. No harassment, hate speech, or misleading engagement tactics like bots, fake followers, or fake reviews.

4. What We Ask of Ourselves

We hold SMTHG to the same standard we hold everyone else to.

Equal treatment. We don't discriminate in hiring, curation, or any platform decision, as prohibited by applicable federal, state, and local law, and we choose to work with inclusive companies as a matter of policy, not preference.

Conflicts of interest. We disclose any personal or financial interest that could influence a platform decision.

Data privacy. We handle personal information as described in our Privacy Policy.

Anti-bribery. We don't offer or accept bribes to any government official or in any commercial dealing, consistent with the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and other applicable anti-bribery law.

No retaliation. We won't retaliate against anyone who reports a concern in good faith under Section 6.

5. US Marketplace Compliance

The INFORM Consumers Act requires an online marketplace to collect and verify identity information from a high-volume third-party seller — one with 200 or more separate sales and at least $5,000 in revenue in a 12-month period — and, for a seller who also crosses $20,000 in annual revenue, to publish certain seller-identity information to consumers. It also requires us to give Customers a clear way to report a suspicious Designer to us. Once smthg. meets the Act's threshold as an online marketplace, we're required to do all of this, and we will.

6. Reporting a Concern

If you believe this Code has been violated, tell us at contact@smthg.us, including anonymously where practicable. We keep reports confidential to the extent possible, won't retaliate against a good-faith report, and review and investigate every report, acting where appropriate under the agreement that applies to them.

7. Enforcement

We may request evidence of compliance with this Code, audit or verify it, and remove a listing, suspend an account, or require remediation where a violation is found. A violation may also result in suspension or termination of a Designer's, Influencer's, or team member's relationship with SMTHG, as set out in the agreement that applies to them.

We do our best to vet every Designer and Influencer against this Code, but we can't independently verify everything they tell us. Responsibility for actually meeting this Code, and for complying with the law, stays with the Designer or Influencer — our reviewing, approving, or featuring a listing doesn't make SMTHG legally responsible for their independent actions or products.
