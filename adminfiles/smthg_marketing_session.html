03What the strategy already says

Positioning (SMTHG — Product Strategy, §3)

  • Category: community-led social commerce for fashion.
  • Core promise: Discover new designers. Find pieces you love. Show your style.
  • Brands: relevant reach, sales, ongoing customer relationships. Creators: discovery, self-expression, income from attributable sales. Shoppers: finding things they love, with the option to collect, share and take part.
  • Initial audiences come primarily through participating influencers; targeted advertising is a possible additional channel. The first priority segment is still to be learned.

Two acquisition paths (§4)

  • Commerce-led: influencer post → personal affiliate link → single-drop Shopify storefront → purchase. Joining the community is optional and never competes with checkout.
  • Event-led: event promotion → community registration → access to the live event → first participation.

Launch targets (§4)

  • 1–3 curated brand drops, at least one commercially successful · about $20,000 GMV · influencer attribution and commission validated.
  • At least 500 community members · at least one live event.

The loop (§2)

  • Brands supply products and stories → creators introduce them to relevant audiences → visitors discover and buy → community deepens relationships → new drops and relationships bring future purchases. Each drop leaves a bigger audience for the next one.

05Community – what was worked out

  • Spaces bring people together around brands, styles, interests and events. Brands share their creative process and keep a relationship with their audience; members exchange ideas and feedback. (Product Strategy, Community and curation)
  • Collections let anyone gather products, images and references around their own taste — personal, public or collaborative. A creator’s shoppable selection uses the same model.
  • Participation is open to people with taste and curiosity, including those without an established audience. Recognition, relationships and discovering fashion give value beyond affiliate income.
  • For the MVP, community work focuses on recruitment and first participation around the live event. A self-running community comes later.
  • So-Me-Space: one shared community with Brand Space, Style Space, Events and the feed. Community is a layer inside the three roles (brands, influencers, consumers), never a fourth role. Social Commerce
  • MVP stack: Circle for the community, Shopify for single-drop storefronts. Reference communities studied: Glucose Guide, Oprah Daily Insider, Buy Women Built. SMTHG Circle
  • Constraint principle: close each role’s biggest pain with a constraint (reference: Breeze, one daily selection at 19:00). Close the pain
  • Internal review of the growth dynamics (August–September 2026): the drop as a live event is the core mechanic; hide stock counters and member counts until the numbers are strong; no daily unread badges at launch; designers replying within 24 hours is the cheapest retention tool; give buyers who miss a drop a path to the next one; the community needs value of its own before it sells.
  • Same review: the community cannot replace influencer seeding at launch; paid influencer seeding stays the main acquisition channel. Size each drop below the waitlist so it sells out.
  • Tone of voice: honest, consistent, accessible luxury; no forced slang. Tone of Voice

06What is missing – questions for Wednesday

  • Channels and order: which channels run for a drop, in which order — influencer posts, @smthg.us, email list, Circle event, paid ads, fashion week presence?
  • Miami Fashion Week: what exactly happens there for the Founding Brands, who is on site, which creators wear which drop?
  • Paid ads: the budget model has no advertising spend in year 1 (smthg_budget.html; it starts in year 2). Do we run "minimal paid ads" for the pilot, on which platform, with what budget, and who pays?
  • Funnel after the post: what does a shopper who does not buy on day one get — email, Circle invite, next drop alert? (Sofi&Co: US buyers need a full funnel.)
  • Creators per drop: how many we contact, how many we need posting, what brief and timeline they get, and who runs it.
  • Live event: one event is a launch target — what is it, when, and how it is promoted.
  • Joint drops: how 10 brands launching together cross-promote each other in practice.
  • Measurement: which few numbers we report to brands after a drop (reach, clicks, sales per creator, new members).
  • Open decision: creator attribution window — 30 days (community spec) or 45 days matched to returns (earlier deck). Not decided.
  • Deliverable: a one-page launch marketing plan we can send to every Founding Brand.
