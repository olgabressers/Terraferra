EU AI Act — Transparency Requirements

Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026. Covers any company whose products or services are used by EU users — including US-incorporated platforms.

  • In force: 2 August 2026 ↗
  • Regulation: EU AI Act, Article 50 — Transparency Obligations for Providers and Deployers
  • Who is covered: Any platform, brand, or tool used by people in the EU — including non-EU companies
  • Penalty: Up to €15,000,000 or 3% of global annual turnover — whichever is higher (Article 99.4(g)). For startups, Art. 99.6 caps at the lower of the two: at $10K MRR, that cap is ≈ $3,600. The real risk is an order to stop serving EU users, not the fine size.
  • SMTHG role: Deployer — using AI tools built by others (ChatGPT, Midjourney, FASHN.AI, Kivisense) and publishing the outputs

Campaign Images & Video
Required
Law — Art. 50.2 + 50.4
Realistic AI-generated images need a visible disclosure if they could be mistaken for real photography. Machine-readable metadata (C2PA) is the AI tool's obligation — visible label is SMTHG's.
What this means for SMTHG
Any AI image posted publicly — Instagram, website, app — needs a label if it looks like real photography.
Action

  • Midjourney: Use Instagram's native AI label (Advanced Settings when posting) + "Created with AI" in caption. Mandatory on every AI image posted publicly.
03
Marketing Copy & Product Descriptions
Action needed
Law — Art. 50.4
AI-generated text published publicly must disclose AI origin. Exception: substantive human editorial review before posting removes the requirement entirely.
What this means for SMTHG
Edit AI-drafted copy before publishing. A real edit — not a rubber-stamp — removes the disclosure obligation.
Action
Workflow: AI draft → human edit → post. Never auto-publish AI copy without a review step.

CLARIFICATION
What exactly must be disclosed — and how?
Three common questions: visible vs machine-readable, avoiding ugly banners, and AI generation vs AI editing.
Question
Machine-readable tag vs visible label — which is required for a website image?
Answer
Machine-readable (Art. 50.2) — the AI tool's obligation, not SMTHG's. OpenAI etc. embed C2PA metadata in the file. Handled automatically when using compliant tools.

Visible to consumers (Art. 50.4) — only for content that could be mistaken for real: realistic images of people or scenes that create a false impression. Stylized or abstract AI visuals: no visible disclosure required.
Question
Will disclosure look ugly? Do I have to put a banner on every image?
Answer
No banner on every image. The law requires a clear and distinguishable disclosure — it does not specify format. Common compliant approaches used by fashion brands:
  • Small corner badge: a discreet "AI" or "AI-generated" chip in the bottom-right of the image (6–8px text, light opacity) — invisible at a glance, present on hover or inspection.
  • Caption / alt text: "AI-generated image" in the product description below the photo.
  • Site-wide footer note: "Some visuals on this site were created with AI" — acceptable for general stylized imagery.
  • Instagram AI label: Instagram's native label is small and non-intrusive. Already standard in fashion.

For realistic model images: badge or caption is cleanest.
Question
Is there a difference between a 100% AI-generated image and a real photo edited with AI?
Answer
Yes — the law distinguishes "generated" from "manipulated."

100% AI-generated image: machine-readable metadata (tool's job). Visible label if result looks realistic.

AI-modified product photo (background swap, lighting fix): no visible disclosure required — minor editing, no false impression of a person.

AI clothing swap on a real person (VTO result, AI styling on a real model): treated as deepfake — visible disclosure required.

Rule: real person's likeness + AI changed what they appear to wear or do → disclose. Product-only editing, no person → no visible disclosure.

What this means for SMTHG
How to solve it
Art. 50.2 + 50.4 — Synthetic image disclosure VTO output is an AI-generated image. Visible disclosure is required wherever it is shown publicly.
Impact VTO result shown only to the shopper = private, no disclosure needed. VTO result in reviews, social proof sections, or shareable links = visible label required.
Action Add "AI Try-On Preview" label on any VTO image shown in a public context. If users can share results: append "Created with AI try-on" to the caption automatically.
Art. 50.3 — Biometric processing disclosure Before processing a user's photo with AI, SMTHG must inform them: photo is processed by AI, which vendor, what for.
Impact A short text notice must appear above the photo upload button — before the shopper can upload.
Action Add text above the upload button: "Your photo is sent to [vendor] AI to generate a try-on preview and is not stored after processing." Include a link to the vendor's privacy policy.
GDPR — Data processing (Art. 28) Accepting the vendor's ToS at sign-up satisfies GDPR Art. 28. No separate contract to negotiate or sign.
Impact No extra paperwork. Sign up, accept ToS — same as OpenAI, Stripe, any SaaS API.
Action When signing up: check the vendor's Privacy Policy — are photos stored after processing? If not stored: nothing else to do. If retained: confirm data residency (EU servers or SCCs).

Required
Law — Art. 50.5
All disclosures must be shown at first interaction — not buried in ToS or a help article. Must be readable and accessible (sufficient contrast).
What this means for SMTHG
Disclosures cannot be in a footer or settings page. Chatbot disclosure = message #1. VTO notice = before the upload button. Image labels = visible on load, not hover-only.
Action
This is a placement rule — no extra work beyond implementing each disclosure correctly per the cards above.

06 — Penalties & Scope
Maximum fine
€15,000,000
Or 3% of global annual turnover — whichever is higher. Article 99.4(g).
Who is covered
Any company with EU users
Applies to non-EU incorporated companies (US, UK, etc.) if their product is used in the EU. SMTHG is covered from day one of EU user activity.
In force since
2 August 2026
These transparency obligations applied first among all AI Act requirements. No grace period for Article 50.
Realistic fine for a startup — Art. 99.6 (SME cap)
For SMEs and startups, Article 99.6 caps the fine at the lower of the two amounts — €15M or 3% of turnover. At SMTHG's revenue stage, 3% of annual revenue is always the lower figure, and becomes the ceiling.
Annual Revenue (SMTHG) 3% of Turnover Max fine (SME cap)
$120K (= $10K MRR milestone) $3,600 ≈ $3,600
$1M (early traction) $30,000 ≈ $30,000
$5M (Series A stage) $150,000 ≈ $150,000
$100M+ (large company) $3M — 3% cap exceeded by €15M €15,000,000
In practice: EU regulators typically issue a compliance order first — not an immediate fine. For a startup at SMTHG's stage, the financial fine is relatively small. The real risk is an order to stop processing EU user data until compliance is achieved — which could block EU market access entirely.
