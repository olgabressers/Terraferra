EU AI Act — Transparency Requirements

Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026. Covers any company whose products or services are used by EU users — including US-incorporated platforms.

  • In force: 2 August 2026 ↗
  • Regulation: EU AI Act, Article 50 — Transparency Obligations for Providers and Deployers
  • Who is covered: Any platform, brand, or tool used by people in the EU — including non-EU companies
  • Penalty: Up to €15,000,000 or 3% of global annual turnover — whichever is higher (Article 99.4(g)). For startups, Art. 99.6 caps at the lower of the two: at $10K MRR, that cap is ≈ $3,600. The real risk is an order to stop serving EU users, not the fine size.
  • SMTHG role: Deployer — using AI tools built by others (ChatGPT, Midjourney, FASHN.AI, Kivisense) and publishing the outputs

01
Customer Service & Chatbots
Required
Law — Art. 50.1
Any AI system designed to interact directly with people must inform the user they are talking to AI. This must happen at the first interaction, before the conversation continues. Exception: only if it is "obvious" to any reasonable person that the interaction is with an AI.
What this means for SMTHG
Every AI chatbot deployed for CS — on website, in-app, or via Instagram DM automation — must identify itself as AI in its first message. Applies whether the bot handles designer onboarding, shopper queries, or order tracking.
How to solve it
  • Configure the bot's opening message to include: "Hi, I'm SMTHG's AI assistant."
  • In Intercom / Zendesk / any CS tool: set this in the bot's welcome step — one config change, no code.
  • Add this as a mandatory line in the bot script template before any SMTHG chatbot goes live.
02
Campaign Images & Video
Required
Law — Art. 50.2 + 50.4
Art. 50.2: AI tool providers (OpenAI, Midjourney) must embed machine-readable metadata (C2PA standard) in generated images and video. Art. 50.4: Deployers publishing that content must add a visible, human-readable disclosure if the content could be mistaken for real photography or footage.
What this means for SMTHG
Any AI-generated campaign image, product visual, or lookbook shot posted to Instagram, the website, or the app needs a visible disclosure if it looks like real photography. Machine-readable metadata is the AI tool's obligation — visible disclosure is SMTHG's responsibility as publisher.
How to solve it — no Adobe
  • ChatGPT / DALL-E: OpenAI embeds C2PA metadata automatically. Machine-readable requirement met by the tool.
  • Midjourney: No C2PA embedded. Use Instagram's native AI label (Advanced Settings when posting) + add "Created with AI" in caption.
  • Internal rule: AI label is mandatory on every AI-generated image posted publicly — not case-by-case.
C2PA standard: c2pa.org
03
Marketing Copy & Product Descriptions
Action needed
Law — Art. 50.4
AI-generated text published for public consumption must disclose its AI origin. Exception: if a human applied substantive editorial review before publication, disclosure is not required. The review must be genuine editing — not just approving an AI draft unchanged.
What this means for SMTHG
AI-drafted Instagram captions, brand descriptions, campaign copy, email subject lines, and app UI text all fall under this rule when published to EU users. Auto-publishing AI copy without human review requires a disclosure label.
How to solve it
  • Apply human review to all AI-drafted copy before posting — a real edit, not rubber-stamp. This removes the disclosure requirement entirely.
  • Add a "copy reviewed" checkpoint to the content SOP: AI drafts → edit → approve → post.
  • Never schedule AI-generated captions to auto-post without a review step in the workflow.
04
Creator & Influencer Content on Platform
Platform policy
Law — Art. 50.4
Creators and influencers who post AI-generated content are themselves deployers and must disclose it. SMTHG as a platform hosting that content is not directly liable for creator uploads — but should have policy in place and tools to support disclosure.
What this means for SMTHG
Influencers posting AI-generated looks, brand collabs, or product videos on SMTHG must label them. Without platform policy and tooling, SMTHG could face reputational and operational risk if the platform becomes associated with unlabeled synthetic content.
How to solve it
  • Creator Agreement: Add clause requiring influencers to disclose AI-generated content at upload — before launch.
  • Onboarding: Include one-page guide on what requires disclosure and how to label it.
  • Product roadmap: Add an "AI-generated" toggle to the post creation flow. Creator checks it; platform shows the label automatically.
  • No enforcement burden today — policy + roadmap item is sufficient at MVP stage.
CLARIFICATION
What exactly must be disclosed — and how?
Three common questions: visible vs machine-readable, avoiding ugly banners, and AI generation vs AI editing.
Question
Machine-readable tag vs visible label — which is required for a website image?
Answer
Machine-readable only (Art. 50.2) — the AI tool's obligation, not SMTHG's. OpenAI, Midjourney, etc. must embed C2PA metadata in the image file. Consumers cannot see it. SMTHG benefits from this automatically when using compliant tools.

Visible to consumers (Art. 50.4) — only required for content that could be mistaken for real: realistic images of real people, faces, or scenes that create a false impression. This is the "deepfake" clause. If the AI image is clearly stylized or abstract, visible disclosure is not required.

Bottom line: Stylized AI fashion visuals on your website → machine-readable metadata (handled by the tool). Realistic AI model photos that look like real people → add a visible label.
Question
Will disclosure look ugly? Do I have to put a banner on every image?
Answer
No banner on every image. The law requires a clear and distinguishable disclosure — it does not specify format. Common compliant approaches used by fashion brands:
  • Small corner badge: a discreet "AI" or "AI-generated" chip in the bottom-right of the image (6–8px text, light opacity) — invisible at a glance, present on hover or inspection.
  • Caption / alt text: "AI-generated image" in the product description below the photo.
  • Site-wide footer note: "Some visuals on this site were created with AI" — acceptable for general stylized imagery.
  • Instagram AI label: Instagram's native label is small and non-intrusive. Already standard in fashion.

For realistic model images: the badge or caption approach is cleanest. Small, tasteful, legally sufficient.
Question
Is there a difference between a 100% AI-generated image and a real photo edited with AI?
Answer
Yes — the law distinguishes between "generated" and "manipulated."

100% AI-generated: no real photo input → machine-readable metadata required (tool's obligation). Visible disclosure if result is realistic.

AI-modified real product photo (background swap, color enhancement, lighting fix): minor editing that doesn't create a false impression about a person → no visible disclosure required. Good practice to add "AI-enhanced" in small text.

AI clothing swap on a real person's photo (e.g. VTO result, or placing AI clothing on a real model): this is manipulation of a real person → treated as deepfake → visible disclosure required.

Practical rule for SMTHG: if a real person's likeness is involved and AI changed what they appear to be wearing or doing → disclose. If it's product-only editing (no person, or clearly abstract) → no visible disclosure required.
05
Virtual Try-On (VTO)
Multiple obligations apply
Law
What this means for SMTHG
How to solve it
Art. 50.2 + 50.4 — Synthetic image disclosure VTO outputs are AI-generated images of a person wearing clothes. The VTO tool provider must embed C2PA machine-readable metadata. The deployer (SMTHG) must add a visible disclosure wherever those images are shown publicly.
Impact VTO result images shown to the shopper are private — no disclosure needed there. But if VTO results are shown in product reviews, social proof sections, or shared by users on social media, visible disclosure is required.

FASHN.AIKivisense — confirm with each vendor whether C2PA is embedded in their output images.
Action
  • Add an "AI Try-On Preview" label on any VTO image shown in a public context (reviews, social proof, shared links).
  • Ask FASHN.AI and Kivisense for their C2PA compliance status.
  • If users can share VTO results externally: auto-append "Created with AI try-on" watermark or caption before sharing.
Art. 50.3 — Biometric processing disclosure Deployers of AI systems used for biometric categorisation must inform individuals before their biometric data is processed. VTO requires a user photo — body shape, proportions, and appearance data are inferred. This is biometric categorisation under Art. 50.3.
Impact Before a shopper uploads a photo to VTO, SMTHG must tell them:
  • Their photo is processed by an AI system
  • Which third-party vendor processes it (FASHN.AI or Kivisense)
  • What the data is used for and how long it is stored
Action
  • Add a disclosure screen before the photo upload step: "Your photo is processed by AI (FASHN.AI) to generate a try-on preview. The image is not stored after your session."
  • Include a link to the VTO vendor's privacy policy.
  • Confirm with FASHN.AI / Kivisense: are user photos stored after processing? If yes — explicit GDPR consent required before upload.
GDPR — Biometric data (Art. 9) Body photos from which physical characteristics are inferred are biometric data under GDPR Art. 9 — a special category requiring explicit consent and a lawful basis. This applies independently of the AI Act. SMTHG must have a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) with any VTO vendor that processes EU user photos.
Impact Without a signed DPA with FASHN.AI or Kivisense, SMTHG cannot lawfully offer VTO to EU users. This is a pre-launch blocker — not a post-launch fix.
Action
  • Before EU launch: obtain a signed DPA from the chosen VTO vendor.
  • Confirm their data residency (EU server or SCCs in place for US processing).
  • Add explicit consent checkbox at VTO entry: "I consent to my photo being processed by AI for try-on purposes."
  • Do not make VTO mandatory — it must be an opt-in feature.
Other Article 50 Paragraphs — Complete Coverage
Art. 50.3
Emotion Recognition
Not applicable — MVP
Law — Art. 50.3
Deployers of AI systems that recognise emotions (from facial expressions, voice, body language, or text) must inform individuals before the system processes them. Separate from biometric categorisation — emotion recognition is its own named category.
Why not applicable at MVP
SMTHG does not plan to deploy emotion recognition in MVP scope. No tool that infers shopper emotional state (joy, frustration, interest) from face, voice, or behaviour is in the architecture. If this changes — for example, AI engagement scoring that reads facial reactions to product images — the obligation activates immediately.
When to revisit
Before adding any AI feature that analyses how users react to content (heatmaps using face tracking, video engagement AI, sentiment inferred from facial expression). At that point: pre-session disclosure required, same format as VTO biometric notice.
Art. 50.2 (audio)
AI-Generated Audio & Voiceovers
Applicable if used
Law — Art. 50.2
Art. 50.2 covers audio alongside images and video. AI tool providers must embed machine-readable C2PA metadata in synthetic audio outputs. Art. 50.4 applies to AI-generated audio that could be mistaken for a real person's voice (audio deepfake) — visible disclosure required.
What this means for SMTHG
Applies if SMTHG uses AI voiceovers for video content, Reels, ads, or platform audio. Tools like ElevenLabs, Murf, or Descript AI voices do not currently embed C2PA by default. A realistic AI voice that sounds like a real person is a "deepfake" under 50.4 and requires visible disclosure.
How to solve it
  • If using AI voiceover in any video: add "AI voiceover" in the video description or end credits.
  • For Instagram Reels: add text overlay or caption note "AI-generated voice".
  • Check whether the audio tool in use supports C2PA export — if yes, enable it.
  • If no AI audio planned at launch: no action needed now.
Art. 50.5
Timing & Accessibility of Disclosures
Required
Law — Art. 50.5
All disclosures under Art. 50.1–50.4 must be delivered at the time of first interaction or first exposure — not buried in ToS, privacy policy, or a help article. They must also meet EU accessibility requirements (equivalent to WCAG 2.1 AA: readable, sufficient contrast, screen-reader compatible).
What this means for SMTHG
  • Chatbot: AI disclosure must be message #1 — not after the user asks a question.
  • VTO: Biometric notice must appear before the photo upload step, not after processing.
  • AI image labels: must be visible at the moment the image loads, not only on hover or scroll.
  • Accessibility: AI label text must have sufficient contrast; add alt text to any AI badge icon.
How to solve it
  • In product design: position all AI disclosures at the start of each user flow — validate in QA.
  • AI badge on images: minimum contrast ratio 4.5:1 (WCAG AA). Add aria-label="AI-generated image" to the badge element.
  • Chatbot: first message is hardcoded, not triggered by user input.
Art. 50.6
Relationship to Other EU Laws
Context — no new obligation
Law — Art. 50.6
Art. 50 does not replace or reduce obligations under other EU laws. GDPR, the Digital Services Act (DSA), the ePrivacy Directive, and national laws all continue to apply independently. AI Act transparency is the floor, not the full picture.
What this means for SMTHG
Complying with Art. 50 alone is not sufficient for full EU legal compliance. GDPR applies to all personal data (covered in VTO section). DSA applies to online platforms — but the heavy obligations (algorithmic transparency reports, independent audits) only apply to "Very Large Online Platforms" with >45M EU monthly active users. SMTHG is far below that threshold.
Why not separately applicable
Art. 50.6 is a cross-reference clause — it creates no new obligation on its own. GDPR compliance (consent, DPA with VTO vendor) is covered in the VTO section. DSA does not impose specific obligations on SMTHG at MVP scale. No separate action required from this paragraph.
Art. 50.7
Codes of Practice (Regulatory)
No operator obligation
Law — Art. 50.7
The EU AI Office will develop voluntary codes of practice to help companies implement C2PA detection, labelling standards, and verification methods. The European Commission may later adopt implementing acts that make specific technical standards mandatory.
What this means for SMTHG
Nothing actionable today. The codes of practice are not yet published (as of August 2026). When released, they will provide more specific guidance on what "machine-readable format" means in practice — which technical standards count as compliant. Following them will be best practice, not a legal requirement unless the Commission converts them into binding implementing acts.
When to revisit
Monitor EU AI Office publications. When codes of practice for C2PA and content labelling are published, review against current SMTHG implementation. The AI Office also runs an innovation sandbox — potentially useful for early-stage platforms building compliant AI features. EU AI Office
06 — Penalties & Scope
Maximum fine
€15,000,000
Or 3% of global annual turnover — whichever is higher. Article 99.4(g).
Who is covered
Any company with EU users
Applies to non-EU incorporated companies (US, UK, etc.) if their product is used in the EU. SMTHG is covered from day one of EU user activity.
In force since
2 August 2026
These transparency obligations applied first among all AI Act requirements. No grace period for Article 50.
Realistic fine for a startup — Art. 99.6 (SME cap)
For SMEs and startups, Article 99.6 caps the fine at the lower of the two amounts — €15M or 3% of turnover. At SMTHG's revenue stage, 3% of annual revenue is always the lower figure, and becomes the ceiling.
Annual Revenue (SMTHG) 3% of Turnover Max fine (SME cap)
$120K (= $10K MRR milestone) $3,600 ≈ $3,600
$1M (early traction) $30,000 ≈ $30,000
$5M (Series A stage) $150,000 ≈ $150,000
$100M+ (large company) $3M — 3% cap exceeded by €15M €15,000,000
In practice: EU regulators typically issue a compliance order first — not an immediate fine. For a startup at SMTHG's stage, the financial fine is relatively small. The real risk is an order to stop processing EU user data until compliance is achieved — which could block EU market access entirely.
