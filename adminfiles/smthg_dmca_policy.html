Effective date: [insert publication date] · Applies to: smthg.us and the SMTHG Community

SMTHG Inc., a Delaware corporation ("SMTHG", "we", "us", or "our"), respects the intellectual property rights of others and complies with the Digital Millennium Copyright Act ("DMCA"). This Policy explains how to submit a copyright complaint, how we respond, and how to submit a counter notice.

Scope: This Policy covers content on smthg.us and the SMTHG Community. It does not cover the SMTHG shop hosted on Shopify — copyright complaints about shop listings are handled directly through Shopify's own copyright policy at shopify.com/legal/tools/report-an-issue/dmca.

1. Filing a Notice of Claimed Infringement

If you believe content on smthg.us or the SMTHG Community infringes your copyright, send a written notice to our Designated Agent (Section 6) that includes:

  • Your name, mailing address, telephone number, and email address;
  • Identification of the copyrighted work you claim has been infringed, or a representative list if multiple works are covered by one notice;
  • The specific URL or other location of the material you claim is infringing, with enough detail for us to locate it;
  • A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use of the material is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law;
  • A statement, made under penalty of perjury, that the information in the notice is accurate and that you are the copyright owner or authorized to act on the owner's behalf; and
  • Your physical or electronic signature.

A notice missing any of these elements may not be actionable under the DMCA.

2. Our Response

On receipt of a valid notice, we will remove or disable access to the reported material and notify the person who posted it that it has been removed and why. We may also remove or disable access to material voluntarily, without a DMCA notice, where we have another legal basis to do so (for example, under our Code of Ethics or Community rules).

3. Counter Notice

If your content was removed and you believe this was the result of mistake or misidentification, you may submit a counter notice to our Designated Agent that includes:

  • Your name, mailing address, telephone number, and email address;
  • Identification of the material that was removed and its location before removal;
  • A statement, made under penalty of perjury, that you have a good-faith belief the material was removed as a result of mistake or misidentification;
  • A statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal court in your district (or, if outside the US, any district where SMTHG may be found) and will accept service of process from the person who filed the original notice; and
  • Your physical or electronic signature.

If we receive a valid counter notice, we will forward it to the person who filed the original complaint. Unless that person notifies us that they have filed a court action seeking to restrain you from the activity, we will restore the removed material not less than 10, and not more than 14, business days after receiving the counter notice.

4. False Claims

Anyone who knowingly materially misrepresents that material is infringing, or that it was removed by mistake, may be liable for damages under 17 U.S.C. § 512(f), including costs and attorneys' fees.

5. Repeat Infringers

We will disable or terminate, in appropriate circumstances, the accounts of users who are repeat infringers.

Registration status: The Designated Agent named above is not yet filed with the U.S. Copyright Office. Safe harbor under 17 U.S.C. § 512(c) only applies once an agent is on file, and it is not retroactive — a complaint that arrives before registration is not protected, even if the agent is registered afterward.

Without a registered agent, SMTHG is not eligible for the DMCA §512(c) safe harbor for content a user posted (e.g. a Community photo or listing). That does not automatically make SMTHG liable for a user's infringement — liability still depends on ordinary secondary-liability standards (e.g. contributory or vicarious infringement) — but it removes the specific statutory defense the safe harbor provides, leaving SMTHG to defend on those more fact-dependent, less predictable grounds instead. Where liability is found, statutory copyright damages run $750–$150,000 per infringed work if the infringement is found willful, plus the copyright owner's attorneys' fees.

Registration is filed online at copyright.gov/dmca-directory. Total cost is $6 (covers every domain SMTHG lists on the same registration — no per-domain fee), the form takes about 10 minutes, and the directory entry goes live within minutes of payment if paid by card (about 7 days if paid by ACH bank transfer). The registration is valid for 3 years, then costs $6 to renew.
