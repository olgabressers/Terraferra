01Brand and founder

  • Sofi created the brand in Bali during the quarantine. The first capsule was a bandeau bikini and cycling shorts that work as sportswear, swimwear and for going out.
  • Idea: a small wardrobe of basics that covers every occasion. Jumpsuits, leggings, sports bras, jackets and swimwear.
  • The brand message is “The Other Side of Fear”: faith bigger than fear, move every day, become the best version of yourself.
  • Before the brand she lived two years in China and acted in films. She is originally from Vladivostok.
  • History: Instagram, then a Russian website and a Moscow showroom, then Shopify with worldwide orders. After the Shopify block in Russia she moved the business to Bali and opened physical stores there. She has now fully left the Russian market and set up the company in the US.
  • Models and bloggers in the brand content are mostly her friends.

02Sales and customers

  • US Shopify store launched in June 2026, after a six-month pause to set up the US company and taxes.
  • Physical store in Canggu, Bali. Shopify online store for the US and worldwide.
  • A friend from the Skywalkers (the team that climbed the Empire State Building) posted the brand jumpsuit. About 1,000 new international followers came from it, and orders started from around the world.
  • Bestsellers: jumpsuits. A set of sports bra, jacket and basic leggings costs about $150. Most prices are under $100; the long-sleeve jumpsuit is $139.
  • Returns are minimal: almost none in Bali, about 2 per 100 online orders in the US.
  • About 20 US orders for Halloween. She will judge the US market after Halloween, Black Friday and New Year.
  • US sales are not yet stable.

03Marketing

  • Meta pixel and paid ads: about $100 per day spent now to train the targeting.
  • In the US, gifting to influencers brings followers but low conversion. Sales need a full funnel: show, subscribe to a newsletter, then an offer.
  • In Russia and Bali, a product posted by a blogger turned straight into sales. The US market works differently.

04Production, stock and shipping

  • Collections are designed and made in Bali. Large batches are produced in China. Russian production (for example the kimono at $200–250 and some jumpsuits at $150–200) is expensive.
  • Per item she can produce 50, 100 or 300 pieces with her own workshops. Third-party factories require 300 pieces per size per colour; in China up to 1,000.
  • Warehouses in Bali and the US. In the US everything is now in California; moving everything to Las Vegas is under consideration. No warehouse in China.
  • Stock physically in the US is small: about 10 pieces per item.
  • New collection on the way: tracksuits. They are heavier, so shipping costs more and a 30% commission is harder on them.
  • New products are added every 3–4 months. Bestsellers stay, the rest rotates.

05Priorities

  • Grow sales volume: target 1,000 sales per month.
  • Brand recognition in the US.
  • Quality of the audience.
  • Community, new contacts and designers, and the experience.

06Position on the SMTHG pilot

  • Interested in taking part and in a joint creative project.
  • Likes that the platform connects brands and concepts, and the idea of limited drops that give a reason to visit the platform.
  • Will not remove bestsellers from her own website: they sell every day.
  • Will not produce a separate exclusive stock or set up a separate warehouse for the platform during the test.
  • The 30% commission needs a price calculation first. US shipping must be included in the price; sales tax is added per state at checkout.
  • Her focus remains her own channel.

  • A handmade corset brand — made in Ukraine, $400–500, about 10 models arriving in around two weeks. Above the pilot price range of up to $250.
