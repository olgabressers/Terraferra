Lekondo Inc., New York. CEO Yeng Tan led AI products at GoDaddy. CTO Mitchell Overfield and core team from Dropbox.

Money

~$3M seed. Led by Verdict Capital — Niko Bonatsos, early Snapchat and Discord. With a16z speedrun and DG Daiwa Ventures.

Speed

Launched 9 January 2026. Version 1.68.0 shipped 9 September — about one release every two days.

Reach

~82,000 users across 10+ countries, 356,000 outfit posts, reported without significant marketing spend. One headline in the same coverage says 100,000 users.

Store

Free, no in-app purchases. Lifestyle and Social Networking. 4.9 from 280 US ratings. iOS, Android and a public Discord.

None published anywhere. The only answer on the record is the data: "high-resolution pre-purchase preference data" positioned upstream of commerce.

03Why the eyes are covered

The bar across the eyes is a product decision. Remove the face and you remove the judgment. Remove the judgment and people post every day.
"The faces are blurred. The usernames mean nothing. The point is the outfit."
"Anonymous by default, so the photo can be the photo and the outfit can be the outfit."
"When the face is absent and the username means nothing, the outfit gets to be the subject."
"What anonymity protects is the photo itself. The expressive power of the clothes, the colors, silhouettes, fabrics, moods."

All four quoted from lekondo.com/lookbook. The bar is visible on every person in every marketing screenshot below.

What it buys them

Volume. 356,000 posts from ~82,000 users in eight months.

Real clothes. What people actually wore on a Tuesday, instead of editorial images nobody owns.

Clean signal. A photo that is not a performance carries a truer preference, which is the asset they raised on.

What it costs them

Nobody to pay. A username that means nothing cannot carry a commission or a brand deal.

No face, no persuasion. Influence runs on a person. There is no person here to build one around.

No transaction. A brand can be tagged on a garment and never reach the member wearing it.

04What they have already built

They are further into community than the funding coverage suggests. Brand communities and aesthetic spaces both exist, both carry member counts, and both were built out of detected garments rather than sold to anyone.

They have

Brand pages with members · topic spaces with members · followers and a Following feed · profiles with a handle and a city · brand tags on individual garments.

They do not have

A checkout · a drop · a designer who owns their page · a creator who earns · a face · a reason for a brand to pay.

Why it matters

The community architecture SMTHG is building has a live comparable running at 82,000 users. The part nobody has built on it is the part that makes money.

06What we can use

The closet builds itself

14 of 35 reviewers praise automatic tracking above everything else. The mechanic that wins is zero effort in: one photo, everything filed.

SMTHG already has the stronger version — a purchase carries the item, the brand, the size and the price with no photo at all.

Nobody asked to be anonymous

Zero of 35 mention faces, anonymity or privacy. They built a product around a fear their own users never voice.

Named identity is not a barrier to adoption. SMTHG can keep the name on the Collection.

Real people beat editorial

8 of 35 write about seeing what actual people wear in other cities. "Street style from users on the other side of the world."

A drop shown on members beats a lookbook. Try-on images from real members are the asset.

Give people words for their taste

5 of 35 say the app taught them to name their own style — "terms and ways to articulate different aesthetics I have never been exposed to."

Naming a member's taste back to them is a retention hook, and it costs nothing to attach to a purchase history.

Me first, everyone else second

The one structural complaint: "seeing to many other peoples content and not just solely based on the individual persons style / closet." Another calls the best part "the single player utility."

Personal space first, feed second. A Collection is one person's, and the community sits around it.

Consumers will not pay

"I pray that this app never gets a pay wall because it is the best." Their whole base is free, and 3 of 35 reject the app outright over AI processing of their photos.

SMTHG charges brands. The member side stays free, which is the right side of this.

08Conclusion

Lekondo is the closest live comparable to the SMTHG community layer, and it has no way to make money from it. Brand pages, aesthetic spaces, followers and a taste graph all exist and work at 82,000 users. There is no checkout, no drop, no designer who owns their page and nobody who earns.
They proved the demand

356,000 outfit posts in eight months with no marketing behind them. People will do the work of documenting clothes if the input costs one photo.

They cannot convert it

A hidden face and a meaningless username leave nobody to pay and nothing to sell. Their own answer is to sell the data instead.

The thesis to answer

Their investors backed the idea that the valuable signal sits before the transaction. The counter is that a purchase is the strongest taste signal there is, and the only one that pays for itself.
