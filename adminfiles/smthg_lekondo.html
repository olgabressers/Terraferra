Competitor Profile

Lekondo

Created: 17 September 2026 · Last updated: 17 September 2026

A New York fashion app that reached 280 App Store ratings at a 4.9 average and a $3M seed round in eight months, built on outfit photos with the faces hidden. Nothing on it is for sale.

Verdict
  • Overlap One column of the five SMTHG claims: taste-based discovery. Lekondo is building a taste graph out of what people already own and wear.
  • No overlap No storefront, no drops, no brand pages, no creator earnings, nothing shoppable. The App Store listing is one sentence about documenting outfits and building a wardrobe.
  • Their bet Anonymity. Their own copy: "Anonymous by default, so the photo can be the photo and the outfit can be the outfit." The face is hidden on every post.
  • Their revenue None published. The investor framing is the data — "high-resolution pre-purchase preference data" positioned as a layer upstream of commerce.
  • Read across Anonymity is why they get volume: 356,000 outfit posts from roughly 82,000 users since January 2026. It is also why there is nobody on the platform to pay.

02What the product does

One photo builds the whole wardrobe. The detector reads every garment in a single outfit shot and files them, which removes the item-by-item photography that kills every other closet app.
  • Post an outfit. The model detects every item in the photo at once, matches them against the closet already built, and adds the pieces that are new.
  • Get a breakdown. Colour palette, key descriptors and style tags, from streetwear to vintage.
  • Keep a calendar. The app describes itself as an OOTD calendar — a dated record of what was worn.
  • Link a garment by pasting a product URL or describing the item, then file it in a searchable closet. The link is storage, with no checkout and no affiliate attached.
  • Browse the lookbook — hundreds of thousands of outfits from anonymous people, described on their own page as sorted by aura, with the ability to follow a colour or an aesthetic across other people's wardrobes.
  • Tap a detected style on your own outfit to see what other people wearing that style are posting anywhere in the world.

Features from Lekondo's own lookbook and digital closet pages and the App Store description.

03Why every face is hidden

The bar across the eyes is a product decision. Removing the face removes the judgment, and removing the judgment is what makes people post every day. It also removes every person a commission could be paid to.

Lekondo's own page states it four times, in four different ways:

  • "The faces are blurred. The usernames mean nothing. The point is the outfit."
  • "Anonymous by default, so the photo can be the photo and the outfit can be the outfit."
  • "When the face is absent and the username means nothing, the outfit gets to be the subject."
  • "What anonymity protects is the photo itself. The expressive power of the clothes, the colors, silhouettes, fabrics, moods, conformity, nonconformity, playfulness, comfort."

All four sentences quoted from lekondo.com/lookbook. The device is visible in Lekondo's own App Store marketing screenshot captioned "Explore the global lookbook", where a grey bar sits across the subject's eyes while the full outfit stays sharp.

What the hidden face buys them
  • Posting volume. 356,000 outfit posts from roughly 82,000 users in eight months. The friction a normal feed creates — who will see this, will I be judged, is this good enough to post — is removed at the source.
  • Everyday clothes instead of editorial. Their positioning sets this against Pinterest boards of unworn editorial imagery. What lands in the feed is what people actually wore on a Tuesday.
  • Clean training data. When the photo is not a performance, the preference signal in it is closer to real behaviour. That is the asset they raised on.
  • A defence against the thing that killed fit-pic posting. Their own framing is the spiral of worry about being seen. Anonymity is the answer they sell.
What it costs them
  • Nobody to pay. A username that means nothing cannot carry a commission, an audience, or a brand deal. There is no creator layer to build on top of this.
  • No follower graph. Anonymous posts do not compound into a personality anyone follows, so the network has no influence to sell.
  • No brand relationship. A designer cannot be met, answered, or bought from inside the app.
  • A monetisation gap they have not filled. Nothing on any published page describes how the company earns. The data framing is the only answer on the record.
Anonymity is the exact trade smthg.us makes in reverse: a Collection carries a name, that name carries an audience, and that audience is what earns. The two products solve the same fear with opposite answers

06What it means for SMTHG

Three things transfer, one is a warning. The closet-input mechanic, the anti-performance insight, and the reviewer vocabulary are all usable. The AI backlash in their one-star reviews arrives at SMTHG's door with virtual try-on.
  • One photo, every item. Detecting a whole outfit from a single shot is the mechanic that gets items into a wardrobe at volume. The same problem sits inside SMTHG Collections, and the same answer applies.
  • The fear they identified is real and it is the same fear. People stopped posting clothes because posting became performance. Lekondo answers it by removing the face. SMTHG answers it by making the room small enough that the audience is people with the same taste.
  • The AI objection is live. Two of the four critical reviews reject the app outright for processing outfit photos with a model — "Keep ai out of art spaces." Virtual try-on asks a member for a photo of their own body, which is a larger ask than a photo of a jacket.
  • Their investors funded the pre-purchase layer. The pitch on the record is that the valuable signal sits before the transaction. SMTHG's counter is that a purchase is the strongest taste signal there is, and the only one that pays for itself.
  • A designer already found them. One five-star review is from a fashion designer recommending the app. There is no way for that designer to sell anything there.
